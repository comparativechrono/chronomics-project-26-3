Stage 1 · The locus and the genome

Weeks 1–2about 5 hours

Find your way around the SCN5A locus with the tools you will use all year: the Linux terminal, the reference genome as a FASTA file, the genes as annotation, and the genome browser. By the end you will have located the two variants and the gene's promoter, and understood the one thing that trips everyone up here – the strand.

You will learn
  • shell commands, paths and pipes in the terminal
  • FASTA files and their .fai index; reading a base with samtools faidx
  • GFF3 and BED annotation; 0-based and 1-based coordinates; the + and − strands
  • the genome browser (igv.js), and a REST API with curl and jq

1. The terminal and the project data

Open the Terminal tab. This is a real Linux shell, running in your browser. Your own files live in your home folder (~); the project's data are read-only in /data/examples/scn5a. Make a folder for your work and look at the data:

mkdir -p ~/project/results ~/project/figures
cd ~/project
ls /data/examples/scn5a
ls -lh /data/examples/scn5a/reference

mkdir -p makes folders, cd moves into one, ls lists what is there (-lh: long, human-readable sizes). Read The terminal in the help if any of this is new.

Check

The reference folder has a FASTA file (GRCh38_chr3_SCN5A.fa.gz) with two index files beside it (.fai, .gzi), a genes file (genes_GRCh38.gff3), some BED files, and a dbSNP file.

2. The reference genome

The FASTA file is a slice of human chromosome 3 (genome build GRCh38). To save space only the 1.5 Mb around SCN5A is real sequence; the rest is written as N, but positions are still those of the whole chromosome. The .fai index lets samtools jump to any position without reading the whole file. Read a few bases:

samtools faidx /data/examples/scn5a/reference/GRCh38_chr3_SCN5A.fa.gz chr3:38651516-38651536

That prints 21 bases of chromosome 3, centred on 38,651,526 – the position of the first variant, rs41310749. The middle base is the reference allele. Read the single base to be sure:

samtools faidx /data/examples/scn5a/reference/GRCh38_chr3_SCN5A.fa.gz chr3:38651526-38651526
samtools faidx /data/examples/scn5a/reference/GRCh38_chr3_SCN5A.fa.gz chr3:38650537-38650537
Check

The reference base at chr3:38,651,526 is G (rs41310749 is G→A), and at chr3:38,650,537 it is A (rs41310239 is A→G). These are the two variants of your project.

3. The genes, and the strand

The genes of the locus are in genes_GRCh38.gff3 (GFF3: one feature a line – gene, transcript, exon – with start, end and strand). Find SCN5A:

grep -P "\tgene\t" /data/examples/scn5a/reference/genes_GRCh38.gff3 | grep "Name=SCN5A"
head -3 /data/examples/scn5a/reference/SCN5A_exons.bed

The gene line gives SCN5A's span and, in the 7th column, its strand: -. SCN5A is on the minus strand, so it is read from high positions to low. Its first exon (exon 1 in the BED) is at the top of the gene, around chr3:38,649,687 – that is the transcription start site (TSS).

Why the strand matters here

Both your variants (38,650,537 and 38,651,526) are at higher positions than the TSS (38,649,687). On the plus strand that would be "after" the gene – but SCN5A runs the other way, so these positions are before the gene: they are in its promoter, about 850 and 1,840 bases upstream of the start. Get this straight now and the rest of the project makes sense.

Check

SCN5A spans chr3:38,548,057–38,649,743 on the − strand. Its neighbour SCN10A (find it the same way) is just above it, around chr3:38,696,802–38,816,286, also on the − strand. Your two variants sit in the SCN5A promoter, between the gene and SCN10A.

4. See it in the genome browser

Open the Genome browser tab. It opens on this project's genome, at the promoter. You should see the genes track, the SCN5A exons, and – in orange – the two variants. Type a position in the box to move, drag to pan, use + / − to zoom.

Your turn

Find the two variants in the browser and zoom until you can see the single bases. Which is closer to the gene? Where is the TSS relative to them? Sketch the layout (gene, TSS, the two variants, SCN10A) in your logbook – you will refer to it all year.

5. Ask a database: the REST API

The terminal can talk to public databases over the web. Ensembl has a REST API; curl fetches a web address and jq reads the JSON that comes back. Look up rs41310749:

curl -s "https://rest.ensembl.org/variation/human/rs41310749?content-type=application/json" | jq '{name, most_severe_consequence, MAF, minor_allele}'
Check

Ensembl calls rs41310749 a regulatory_region_variant, with a minor allele A at a frequency (MAF) of about 0.14. (Try rs41310239 too: its minor allele is G, about 0.11.) "Regulatory region" is a first hint that these variants are in something that controls a gene – which stage 2 looks at.

You can do the same in the Databases tab (Ensembl card, "Variant / VEP"), which also gives you the code to repeat the query. Both ways matter: the tab is quick, the terminal can be put in a script.

For your logbook
  • The positions, reference and alternate alleles of both variants, and their distance from the TSS.
  • In your own words: why "upstream" of SCN5A means higher coordinates.
  • Your sketch of the locus.
