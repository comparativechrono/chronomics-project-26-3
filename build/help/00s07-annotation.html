Stage 7 · What is in the block?

Weeks 7–8about 5 hours

You have a ~50 kb block. What is actually in it – which other variants, and what do they touch? Annotate the block with Ensembl's Variant Effect Predictor, find the regulatory element the two variants sit in, and meet the neighbourhood that makes this locus famous for the heart.

1. The consequence of the two variants

Ask Ensembl's VEP what each variant does. In the terminal with the REST API (or use the Databases tab, Ensembl → Variant/VEP):

cd ~/project
for rs in rs41310749 rs41310239; do
  echo "== $rs"
  curl -s "https://rest.ensembl.org/vep/human/id/$rs?content-type=application/json" \
    | jq -r '.[0] | .most_severe_consequence, (.regulatory_feature_consequences // [] | .[].biotype)'
done
Check

Both are upstream_gene_variant of SCN5A (the promoter). rs41310749 also overlaps a regulatory feature of biotype enhancer – Ensembl's regulatory build marks the spot as an enhancer. So the variant does not change a protein; it sits in a switch.

2. Annotate the whole block

Take the block SNPs you found in stage 5 (r² ≥ 0.8), give them rsIDs, and see what kinds of thing they are. First list the block positions and name them from dbSNP:

bcftools view -H -v snps -r chr3:38631501-38682101 results/hprc_split.vcf.gz 2>/dev/null | head -1 >/dev/null
# name the block variants from dbSNP and keep those in your LD list
bcftools annotate -a /data/examples/scn5a/reference/dbsnp157_scn5a_2Mb.vcf.gz -c ID \
  -r chr3:38631501-38682101 /data/examples/scn5a/1000g/1kgp_scn5a_2Mb.vcf.gz 2>/dev/null \
  | bcftools view -H -v snps | awk '$3 ~ /^rs/ {print $2, $3, $4"/"$5}' | head -30

In R you can join these names to your ld_eur.csv (match on position) and list the block's rsIDs. Then look a few up in the VEP card. You will find the block is mostly intron variants of SCN5A and intergenic variants in the gap towards SCN10A – none of them change a protein.

Check

The block's variants are regulatory/intronic/intergenic, not coding. The haplotype is a regulatory haplotype: whatever it does, it does by changing how genes are switched, not by changing a protein's sequence.

3. The neighbourhood: SCN5A and SCN10A

An enhancer in the next gene

Your block reaches from inside SCN5A, across its promoter, into the DNA before SCN10A. This gap is not empty: it holds enhancers – switches that control SCN5A from a distance. The best known is inside SCN10A itself: van den Boogaard et al. (2012) showed that a common variant there changes a T-box binding site and tunes SCN5A expression and the heart's conduction. The whole SCN5A–SCN10A region is a cluster of heart-conduction switches, and your promoter haplotype is one part of it. Stage 10 will show that the strongest disease signal at this locus is up at the SCN10A end – so keep asking whether your promoter haplotype matters on its own, or only because it rides along with that larger signal.
