A first session with the tools

Fifteen minutes to see every tab of the workbench once, with the project's own data. Each step has a button that does it for you – but type the commands too.

1 · The terminal

The Terminal tab is a Linux shell (bash) with real bioinformatics programs, running in this page. The project's data are in /data/examples/scn5a:

ls /data/examples/scn5a
ls /data/examples/scn5a/hprc
head -3 /data/examples/scn5a/hprc/hprc_samples.tsv

Type help for the list of programs, and man samtools for one of them. Stage 1 of the guide starts here.

2 · A notebook

The Notebook tab opens with start-here.ipynb, an R notebook. Click into its first grey cell and press Shift+Enter: the code runs, its output appears below, and the next cell is selected. R starts in the background; the first run waits for it, a few seconds.

3 · Your files

Your own files live in your home folder, ~ (/home/user). The terminal, the notebooks and the Files tab all see the same files: what a command writes, a notebook can read a moment later.

mkdir -p ~/first && cd ~/first
cut -f 2,4 /data/examples/scn5a/hprc/hprc_samples.tsv | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn > populations.txt
head populations.txt

4 · The other tabs

  • Genome browser – the genome, genes and variants. (Genome browser)
  • Databases – Ensembl, NCBI, UCSC, UniProt and Europe PMC, with forms; each search can go into a notebook as code. (Databases)
  • 3D viewer – protein structures from the PDB or AlphaFold. – coloured by AlphaFold's confidence: orange means "not confident", typical of the long floppy parts of proteins. (3D viewer)
  • IDE – RStudio for R and Spyder for Python, for when a script suits the job better than a notebook. (The IDE tab)
  • AI – a chat and an agent, with a model and a key of your choice. It can be wrong: check what it says, and say in your report if you used it. (AI assistant)

5 · Keep your work

Everything you make is kept in this browser on this computer. My files (top right) downloads all your files as a .zip; do it once a week. Keep your work has the details.
